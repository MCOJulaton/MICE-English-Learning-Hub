EFC Unit 10: What Makes a Good Home? Teacher Guide

Timed 120-minute plan, Bloom's and ADDIE map, client answers, Kahoot bank, gestures, and common problems for the A1 "Home Designer" unit. This site has no login, so treat this page's URL as unlisted, not truly private. Do not share the link with students.

Lesson at a Glance

Idea: students are home designers. They learn about 55 words (rooms, things in rooms, home features) and the frames I like / I want / I don't want ... because ..., then help a client choose a home with a small budget of coins, and finally design their own perfect home and present it.

Path: LOOK, LEARN, SAY WHY, HELP A CLIENT, DESIGN MY HOME, WRITE, SPEAK, PRESENT. Every activity can be done by clicking. Nothing needs typing. The coin rule is the only new idea, and the teacher shows it first with Mina. Every home starts empty, so students put every item in themselves.

Student page: index.html (9 sections plus the cover and finish screens). Worksheet to print: _deliverables/EFC-Unit10-University-Worksheet.docx or .pdf (black and white, 2 pages: vocabulary, dream home plan, writing, partner feedback). A simpler color version with a drawing page is EFC-Unit10-My-Perfect-Home-Worksheet.docx or .pdf.

Audio: the Listen buttons use the browser's built-in English voice (American). The old housing-policy recordings are not used. The files are still in assets/audio/comm-unit10.

Clients and Answers

Every home starts empty. Students choose each item themselves. A home needs a bedroom, a bathroom, and a kitchen (a checklist item on every client screen). The first bedroom, the first bathroom, and a small kitchen are free. Costs for everything else: extra bedroom 1, extra bathroom 1, big kitchen 2, near university 2, living room 1, study room 1, balcony 1, garden 2, pool 3. The site does not allow spending more than the coins.

ClientCoinsNeeds (cost)Free coinsExample reason sentence

Noi7Big kitchen (2), garden (2) = 43 (living room, study room, balcony)I choose a big kitchen because Noi likes cooking.

Your turn: after Mina, pairs choose the Ploy family or Noi (Partner A one, Partner B the other works well, then they tell each other). The site records this as a separate activity from Mina. Students who finish early click "Try ..." to do the other client.

Mina has 2 free coins after her needs and the basics. It is a real choice: ask "Why a balcony?" "A living room is 1 coin. Is it OK?"

Tell your partner: after the reasons, a box shows the sentences to say: "This is Mina. She wants a home near the university and a study room. I choose a study room because Mina studies a lot."

Run it at about minute 50, after the words and sentences. Create the questions in your free Kahoot account. Text only, 20 seconds each, no images. Mark the green answer as correct.

Optional Extension: Find a Roommate

After Section 6, two students compare their homes: "Do you want a pool?" "Yes, I do." / "No, I don't." They decide if they can share a home. This adds one new frame (Do you want ...?), so use it only with a strong class.

Support for A1 Students

  • One instruction at a time. Say it, show it, wait. Use the same words: "Look. Click. Listen. Say it."
  • Model first. Do Mina on the projector first, then pairs try the Ploy family or Noi. The coin rule is the only new idea.
  • No wrong answers in Sections 1, 6, and 8. In the coin game, a design that spends all the coins and meets the needs is a good design.
  • Wrong clicks in the games show "Try again." Students keep clicking. The score counts first-try answers only.
  • Spelling: American English (favorite, color).

Final Project Link

"My Perfect Home" is the individual practice for the Unit 11 project, Design a Home and Give a Presentation (groups of 3 to 4). Students bring their Unit 10 design, worksheet, and script. Unit 11 itself was not changed.

Presentation model (Section 8): Hello. This is my perfect home. It has three bedrooms. It has two bathrooms. It has a big kitchen. It has a garden. My favorite room is my bedroom. I like it because it is comfortable. Thank you.

Common Problems

Students do not understand the coins.
Fix: use real coins or paper coins on your desk for Mina. Show: "2 coins. Pay." Then let them click.
"No more coins" appears.
Fix: that is the game. Ask "Which thing do you not need?" and have them click Start again or change a choice.
Students do not say anything.
Fix: use click answers first, then repeat the sentence together, then in pairs.
Listen button is silent.
Fix: the voice comes from the browser. Check the device volume. Chrome and Safari work best.
A student skipped Design Your Home.
Fix: Sections 7 and 8 show a button to go back to Section 6, where they design the home.
