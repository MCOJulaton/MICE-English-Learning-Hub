EFC Unit 11: Design a Home Teacher Guide

Timed 120-minute plan, Bloom's and ADDIE map, answers, scoring guide, gestures, and common problems for the A1 group project. This site has no login, so treat this page's URL as unlisted, not truly private. Do not share the link with students.

Lesson at a Glance

Idea: in Unit 10 each student designed their own home. In Unit 11 the group designs one home. Students learn to say where things are (in, on, next to, between, behind, in front of, across from), to talk about a neighborhood, and to agree and disagree politely. The unit ends with the graded final group presentation. This is the only graded presentation in Units 10 and 11.

Path: REVIEW, WHERE IS IT?, OUR NEIGHBORHOOD, AGREE AND DISAGREE, PLAN OUR HOME (shared coins), FLOOR PLAN, OUR SCRIPT, FINAL PRESENTATION. Everything is done by clicking. Nothing needs typing.

Groups: 3 or 4 students. Form the groups before Section 4 (Agree and Disagree). Sections 1 to 4 can be done by each student alone or in pairs. From Section 5 on, one device per group works best, because the group has one home. Everyone must be able to see the screen.

Student page: index.html (8 sections plus the cover and finish screens).

Link to Unit 10: students can bring their Unit 10 worksheet. Each student offers one or two ideas from their own dream home. The group decides together. The site does not copy Unit 10 homes, because each Unit 11 home is new and shared.
Audio: the Listen buttons use the browser's built-in English voice (American). The old textbook recordings are not used in this unit. The files are still in assets/audio/comm-unit11.

Answers

Section 2: Where Is It? (quiz)

The picture shows a ball and a box. The answer is the word for where the ball is: in, on, next to, between (two boxes), behind, in front of. The site gives 3 choices each time.

Section 3: the map

1234
Top sideparkcoffee shopbankuniversity
Bottom sidebus stopsupermarketshopping mallhospital

Across from = the same column (park and bus stop). Next to = the same side, touching. Between = the two middle places on each side (coffee shop between the park and the bank). Every place has an "across from" sentence.

Section 4: Agree and Disagree

Each round has one polite answer with a reason. The wrong choices are rude ("No! Bad idea.") or do not make sense (disagree, but the reason is good). Say: a polite no needs a reason.

Section 5: Plan Our Home

The group has 15 coins. The first bedroom, the first bathroom, and a small kitchen are free. Extra bedroom 1, extra bathroom 1, big kitchen 2, near university 2, living room 1, study room 1, balcony 1, garden 2, pool 3. Everything together costs 17 at most (4 bedrooms, 3 bathrooms, big kitchen, and every feature), so the group must choose. The site does not allow spending more than 15. There is no wrong home. A good home uses the coins and the group can say why.

Section 6: Floor Plan

The house has 6 places: three on the top, a hall, three on the bottom. Each place in the top row is across from the place below it. Only the room types the group chose (bedroom, bathroom, kitchen, plus living room, study room, balcony) go in the house. Garden and pool are outside. The plan is complete when every room is placed and at least 2 "where" sentences exist. Students cannot finish by putting rooms far apart.

Section 7: Script

Group of 4: Student 1 introduces the home, Student 2 tells the features and coins, Student 3 shows the floor plan, Student 4 says why the group agrees and thanks the class. Group of 3: Student 3 does both of the last two parts. The sentences come from the group's own choices, so every group has a different script.

Scoring the Final Presentation (100 points)

Support for A1 Students

  • One instruction at a time. Say it, show it, wait. Use the same words: "Look. Click. Listen. Say it."
  • Use real objects. A ball and a box teach in, on, next to, between, behind, and in front of in one minute. Do this before Section 2.
  • Polite disagreeing is the hard part. Teach it as a pattern: "I do not agree" plus a reason. Say it with a smile. Let students practice with the idea cards in Section 4.
  • Roles in the group: one student clicks, one reads the rules, one writes who said what, one watches the time. Change roles for each section.
  • Wrong clicks show "Try again." Students keep clicking. The score counts first-try answers only.
  • Spelling: American English (favorite, color).

Common Problems

One student does all the talking and clicking.
Fix: give roles (see above). In Section 5, say "Every student gives one idea before you click."
"No more coins" appears.
Fix: that is the game. Ask "Which thing is not so important?" and have the group agree on one change, or click Start again.
The group has no ideas.
Fix: use the "Talk like this" box in Section 5 and the Unit 10 worksheets. Each student reads one idea from their own dream home.
The floor plan will not finish.
Fix: it needs every room placed and at least 2 sentences. Tell students to put two rooms next to each other or across from each other.
Section 6 or 7 asks the student to go back.
Fix: the home and the floor plan are saved on the device used today. Use the same device and browser for Sections 5, 6, and 7. If a student changes device, repeat Section 5 first.
Students only read the script.
Fix: that is OK at A1. Ask them to look up at the class for the first and last lines and to point at the floor plan when they say "next to" and "between".
Listen button is silent.
Fix: the voice comes from the browser. Check the device volume. Chrome and Safari work best.
