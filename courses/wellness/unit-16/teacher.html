Wellness Unit 16: The Wellness Feature Teacher Guide

How to run the integrated final unit: a timed plan, what students use the site for, answers, assessment links to the TQF 3, and common problems. This site has no login, so treat this page's URL as unlisted, not truly private. Do not share the link with students.

Lesson at a Glance

Idea: the site is the toolkit for the final output, The Wellness Feature: a group video (6 to 8 minutes), a group presentation, and an individual reflection. Students do not write on the site. They write on the printed Group Capstone Sheet and Individual Reflection. The site teaches how a feature is built, what strong information looks like, the guest language from the earlier units, how to answer a guest's real concern, how to plan and film responsibly, and how to answer questions.

Integration: consulting a guest, recommending, explaining, and confirming (Units 9 to 15) become the guest language for target 7. Handling a concern (complaints and problem solving) becomes target 8, "A real consideration", and the way to answer Q&A.

TQF 3 links: Week 15 Presentation Preparation and Peer Rehearsal, and Week 16 Final Wellness Tourism Group Presentation. CLO 2, CLO 4, CLO 6, CLO 7. The site has 8 sections plus the cover and finish screens. The final presentation date shown to students is a setting in js/data.js (FINAL_DATE).

Recommended Plan for a 3-Hour Session

I recommend one hour of teaching, then two hours of group work with checkpoints. Free group time with no structure usually drifts. A short guided hour gives every group the same standard, then the groups use the toolkit when they need it.

Answers

Section 1: True or False

The false statements are: only one student needs to write a target; AI-generated video is allowed; "written by" means who says it on camera. The true statements are: ask permission before filming; every student speaks; the video is about 6 to 8 minutes.

Section 2: model lines

Opening: the welcome line and the "We are Group 3" line. Feature: the steamed herbal balls line, the lemongrass benefits line, the session time line, and the sensitive skin line. Closing: the tired travelers line, the "you can try it" line, and the thank you line.

Section 3: Strong Information

The stronger line is always the specific one (who, how long, what, where). The weak lines use words like "nice", "good", "some", "somewhere". All 10 targets have one weak and one strong line.

Section 4: Guest Language

Each phrase belongs to one of: Ask the goal (what the guest wants), Recommend (based on what you told me, I would recommend), Explain (how long, what happens, benefits), Confirm (book, check details, ask for questions).

Section 5: A Real Consideration

The best response acknowledges the guest, gives accurate information, offers a solution, and checks back. Wrong responses ignore the worry, are impolite, or give a false promise ("Yes, of course, no problem" for pregnancy).

Section 6: Filming rules

OK: asking the manager for permission, recording in a quiet place, asking a therapist for a short tip with permission. Not OK: filming a guest without asking, using an AI-created video, showing private staff prices.

Section 7: Questions

The best answers are specific and honest. If a student does not know, the three phrases in the "If you do not know" box are all acceptable.

The quiz examples (herbal compress massage, Patong, lemongrass) are models only. Groups must research and write real, accurate information about their own activity and venue. Check the facts on the Group Sheets.

Assessment and the TQF 3

  • Final project weight: 10% of the course grade (CLO 6 and CLO 7).
  • Rubric on the site: the six-criterion Wellness Feature Capstone Rubric (Content Accuracy, Wellness Tourism Connection, English Communication, Presentation, Professionalism, Teamwork), scored 4 to 1, total 24.
  • Check before the final: the TQF 3 (Appendix D.2) lists a different Final Presentation rubric with five criteria at 20 points each (Content and Knowledge, Organisation and Structure, Language and Delivery, Visual Presentation and Materials, Q&A Responsiveness). Decide which rubric counts, or how to convert, and tell students once. The site shows the capstone rubric, and Q&A is covered in Section 7 in either case.
  • Presentation length: the TQF 3 describes a 3 to 5 minute presentation with a 2 minute Q&A. The capstone guide describes a 6 to 8 minute video plus a presentation. The site does not state a presentation time limit. Tell students your limit.
  • AI policy: the video must be real footage. No AI-generated video.

Support Tips

  • Model, then release. Do Sections 1 to 3 together. Let groups use Sections 4 to 8 as they need them.
  • One real activity. Push groups to choose something they can actually visit. Real footage is worth more than a perfect script.
  • Specific beats long. Use the Section 3 rule when you read the Group Sheets.
  • Everyone writes. Look at "Written by" on the sheet. No blanks.
  • Venue safety: students should go in groups, tell the venue who they are, and never film guests without permission.

Common Problems

A group cannot choose an activity.
Fix: ask three questions: Can we visit it? Can we explain it in English? Is it Thai or Phuket-specific? If yes to all three, choose it.
One student writes everything.
Fix: check "Written by". Each student must own at least one target, and everyone writes a reflection.
The information is general ("it is good for health").
Fix: ask "Who? How long? Where? What happens first?" until the line is specific.
The group cannot get permission to film.
Fix: they can film a demonstration at school or at home with a friend as the guest, with permission. A real venue and a real practitioner are best, but not required.
Students read the script without looking up.
Fix: use Section 2's tips and the rehearsal checklist. Ask them to look up for the first and last line.
Listen button is silent.
Fix: the voice comes from the browser. Check the device volume. Chrome and Safari work best.
